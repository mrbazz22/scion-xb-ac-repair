Compressor Removal
1
Recover Refrigerant
Connect recovery machine to high and low side service ports. Recover all refrigerant from the system. Do not vent to atmosphere — it's illegal and bad for the ozone.
WARNING: Always wear safety glasses and gloves. Refrigerant can cause frostbite.
2
Remove Engine Under Covers
Remove the rear engine under cover (RH side) and the No. 1 engine under cover to gain access to the compressor.
3
Remove V-Belt
Loosen the tensioner and remove the fan & generator V-belt. Note the routing for reinstallation.
Tip: Take a photo of the belt routing before removal.
4
Disconnect Suction Hose
Remove the bolt and disconnect the suction hose from the compressor. Remove the O-ring from the hose.
NOTICE: Seal openings with vinyl tape immediately to prevent moisture/foreign matter entry.
5
Disconnect Discharge Hose
Remove the bolt and disconnect the discharge hose from the compressor. Remove the O-ring from the hose.
NOTICE: Seal openings with vinyl tape immediately.
6
Remove Compressor
Disconnect the electrical connector. Remove the 2 bolts and 2 nuts. Using an E8 Torx socket, remove the 2 stud bolts and remove the compressor.
Torque spec for later: Stud bolts 9.8 Nm, mount bolts 25 Nm
Receiver Drier Removal
1
Locate Receiver Drier
The receiver drier is located in the high-pressure line, typically near the condenser or on the firewall. Check your specific model configuration.
2
Remove Mounting Hardware
Remove the bracket bolts securing the receiver drier.
3
Disconnect Lines
Remove the line connections. Replace all O-rings. Seal openings immediately with vinyl tape or plugs.
Compressor Installation ✅ COMPLETED
1
Oil Balancing
New compressor comes with 90cc of ND-OIL8. You must drain the difference between 90cc and whatever oil remains in your old system. If old compressor had 30cc remaining, drain 60cc from the new one before installation.
CRITICAL: Too much oil = poor cooling. Too little = compressor damage.
2
Install Compressor
Using E8 Torx socket, install compressor with 2 stud bolts. Then install the 2 bolts and 2 nuts.
Torque: Stud bolts 9.8 Nm (87 in-lbf), Mount bolts 25 Nm (18 ft-lbf)

Expansion Valve Replacement ✅ COMPLETED
CRITICAL FIELD DISCOVERY — Correct Under-Hood Method

The Operation CHARM service manual is WRONG for this job. It shows expansion valve replacement requiring full HVAC unit removal through the dash (glove box, instrument panel, dashboard — 6+ hour job). In reality, the expansion valve on the 2010 Scion xB 2AZ-FE is accessible from under the hood in minutes.

Service Manual vs Reality
Source Method Time Difficulty
Operation CHARM Manual Remove glove box, dash panels, full HVAC unit from firewall 6-8 hours Extreme
Actual Field Method ✅ Under hood: 1 bolt + 2 screws, evaporator pops off 15-30 min Easy
1
Recover Refrigerant
System must be fully evacuated before opening any lines. Recover any remaining refrigerant.
WARNING: Never open AC lines under pressure. System must be fully evacuated.
2
Access from Under Hood (NOT Behind Glove Box)
The expansion valve is mounted on the evaporator core, which sits on the passenger-side firewall. Look straight down from above — you'll see the high/low line plate held by a single 10mm hex bolt.
Correct access: under hood, passenger firewall
DO NOT remove glove box or dash — valve is under hood!
3
Remove the Line Plate (10mm Hex Bolt)
Remove the single 10mm hex bolt holding the plate that secures the high and low pressure lines to the evaporator assembly. The plate will come loose, exposing the connections.
Tool: 10mm socket or wrench
4
Remove Evaporator Mounting Screws (2× T25 Torx)
Two T25 Torx machine screws secure the evaporator assembly to the firewall. Remove both screws — the evaporator assembly will now pop off the firewall freely.
Tool: T25 Torx bit/driver
5
Extract Old Valve, Clean, Install New
With the evaporator loose, you can access the expansion valve directly. Remove old valve, clean mating surfaces, install new GPD 3411860 valve with new O-rings (UAC RS2584 kit) lubricated with ND-OIL8.
IMPORTANT: Valve is directional — check flow arrow (liquid line IN → evaporator OUT)
Torque: 9.8–11 Nm (87–97 in-lbf) — do not overtighten, aluminum threads strip easily
6
Reattach Everything
Pop evaporator back onto firewall, install 2× T25 Torx screws. Reattach line plate with 10mm hex bolt. Use new O-rings on all connections, lubricated with ND-OIL8.
Seal all openings with vinyl tape or plugs if not reconnecting immediately — prevent moisture entry.
✅ Actual Result

This method took under 30 minutes in the field. The service manual's 6-8 hour dash-out procedure would have been unnecessary and damaging. Always check for practical under-hood access first!

Condenser Replacement 🟡 PENDING
Why Replace It?

The condenser sits in front of the radiator and releases heat from the compressed refrigerant. Leaks here are common, especially at the connection points. Replacing the condenser when the system is already open ensures a complete, reliable repair.

1
Remove Old Condenser
Disconnect the refrigerant lines (high and low pressure) from the condenser. Remove the mounting bolts securing the condenser to the radiator support. Carefully lift the old condenser out.
NOTICE: Cap all lines immediately to prevent contamination and moisture ingress.
2
Install New Condenser (UAC CN3755PFC)
Position the new condenser and secure with mounting bolts. Reconnect the refrigerant lines using new O-rings lubricated with ND-OIL8. Torque connections to spec.
Torque: Line connections typically 9.8–11 Nm (87–97 in-lbf)
3
Replace O-Rings
Use new O-rings from the UAC RS2584 kit on every connection. Lubricate with ND-OIL8 before assembly. Do not reuse old O-rings — they are a primary leak point.

Evacuation & Recharge
1
Pull Vacuum
Connect vacuum pump to service ports. Pull vacuum to at least 29 inHg and hold for 30 minutes to remove all moisture and air.
Target: 29-30 inHg vacuum for minimum 30 minutes
2
Vacuum Hold Test
Close valves and shut off pump. Monitor gauge for 15 minutes. If vacuum drops, you have a leak — find and fix it before charging.
3
Charge Refrigerant
Charge system with R-134a. The 2010 xB 2AZ-FE takes approximately 450-550g (15.9-19.4 oz). Use a scale for accuracy.
Spec: Check under hood sticker for exact charge amount
4
Leak Test
Run system and check all connections with leak detector or UV dye. Check especially at compressor hose connections, receiver drier fittings, and expansion valve connections.
5
Test Operation
Start engine, turn AC to max cold. Verify compressor clutch engages (listen for clunk, watch plate spin with pulley). Check vent temperature.
Target vent temp: 40-45°F (4-7°C) at idle on a warm day
Diagnostics & Troubleshooting
⚠️ Critical: Clutch Won't Engage Without Refrigerant

The pressure switch prevents the compressor clutch from engaging when the system is empty (no refrigerant = no pressure = switch open). This is a safety feature, not a fault. You MUST have a proper refrigerant charge before the clutch will engage electrically.

1. Verify Refrigerant Charge First
The ECU will not engage the compressor if refrigerant pressure is too low (leak) or too high (overcharge). Check with manifold gauges. The pressure switch opens below ~25 PSI (low pressure cutoff).

Test: With system charged, pressure should read 30-50 PSI on low side at idle. If gauges show 0 PSI, the switch is open and clutch will not engage — this is normal for an empty system.
2. Check Fuses (A/C MAG Fuse)
Locate the A/C MAG fuse (10A) in the engine compartment fuse box. Also check the IG2 fuse and any AC-related fuses in the interior fuse box behind the dash.

Tip: Use a test light or multimeter — don't just visually inspect. A fuse can look good but have a hairline crack.
3. Test the A/C Relay
The A/C relay is in the engine compartment fuse box. When you press the AC button, you should hear the relay click.

Quick test: Swap the A/C relay with a known-good relay (like the horn relay). If the relay is bad, the clutch won't get power even if everything else is fine.

Direct test: Remove relay, use a jumper wire between pins 30 and 87 (consult relay diagram). This bypasses the control circuit and sends power directly to the clutch coil. Only do this briefly — if clutch works, the problem is upstream (relay, pressure switch, or ECU).
4. Test Pressure Switch
The refrigerant pressure switch is on the high side line near the receiver drier. It prevents compressor operation if pressure is out of range (too low = leak, too high = overcharge).

Test with gauges: If low side reads 0-10 PSI, switch is open (normal for empty system). Charge the system and retest — if clutch still won't engage after proper charge, the switch may be faulty.

Direct test: Unplug the pressure switch connector and jumper the two wires together. This fakes a "pressure OK" signal to the ECU. Only for testing — do not leave jumpered while driving. If clutch engages with jumper, the switch or charge level is the problem.
5. Direct 12V Test at Compressor
Unplug the compressor connector. Apply 12V directly to the clutch coil terminals (typically the two-wire connector on the compressor, not the single wire for the pulley).

You should hear a distinct clunk and see the clutch plate lock to the pulley. If it doesn't engage, the clutch coil is bad (rare on a new compressor, but possible).

WARNING: Only apply 12V for 2-3 seconds. The coil will overheat if energized continuously without the engine running.
6. Check Wiring Harness
Inspect the wiring from the compressor back to the relay and fuse box. Look for chafing, corrosion, or loose connections — especially near the compressor where heat and vibration are highest.

Test: With relay removed, check for 12V at the relay socket pin 30 (constant power) and pin 86 (control power from ECU when AC button pressed).

✅ Diagnostic Flowchart

  2. System charged? → Yes → Check A/C MAG fuse (10A)
  3. Fuse good? → Yes → Test A/C relay (swap or jumper)
  4. Relay good? → Yes → Jumper pressure switch
